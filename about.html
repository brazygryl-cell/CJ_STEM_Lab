Built from a classroom need

Curriculum should reduce teacher workload without reducing teacher judgment.

CJ STEM Lab began with a practical problem: middle-school technology teachers are often given standards and a course title, then expected to design the rest—daily instruction, slides, activities, projects, assessments, differentiation, and pacing—from the ground up.

Teacher-built

Founder perspective

Designed from direct experience across STEM and computer science classrooms.

The founder’s classroom experience spans Grade 6 Intro to STEM, Grade 7 Computer Literacy, Grade 8 Intro to Computer Science, and advanced high-school programming. That range shapes the product’s emphasis on realistic pacing, clear explanations, low-cost hands-on work, Chromebook-based instruction, visible learning targets, project management, and materials a single teacher can actually run.

The goal is not to turn one teacher’s classroom website into a larger website. The goal is to turn tested instructional practices and existing curriculum assets into a coherent product system that other educators and schools can use.

Mission

Equip teachers with complete, adaptable instruction—and give students meaningful work worth explaining.

CJ STEM Lab prioritizes classroom usability, equitable participation, responsible technology use, and clear progression across grade levels. Students should investigate, build, code, create, test, explain, and revise. Teachers should receive the structure needed to make that work manageable.

BUILD

Investigate & engineer

Hands-on science, fair testing, structures, systems, prototypes, design decisions, and evidence.

CODE

Understand & program

Digital systems, algorithms, web development, Python, data, networks, cybersecurity, and debugging.

CREATE

Design & communicate

Human-centered design, AI literacy, creative technology, reflection, documentation, and revision.

Product principles

What should remain true as the platform grows.

Teacher first

The first week of use should reduce preparation burden rather than create a new implementation burden.

Explicit teaching + authentic application

Students receive the concepts and examples they need before they are asked to create independently.

Visible evidence

Hands-on work still connects to learning targets, assessment criteria, revision, and explanation.

Responsible technology

Privacy, accessibility, safety, attribution, verification, and human judgment are product requirements—not fine print.

New Jersey first

Start locally, prove the model, then expand carefully.

The initial Grades 6–8 pathway is being designed around New Jersey standards and implementation realities. A New Jersey-first launch makes it possible to test curriculum quality, teacher usability, school fit, and pilot support before building state-specific crosswalks elsewhere.

Long-term vision

Grades 3–12, built from a stable core.

The long-term product is a learning system where teachers can plan and teach complete courses, students can complete interactive and hands-on work, and administrators can support implementation. Expansion follows evidence: first a coherent Grades 6–8 pathway, then adjacent grade bands and software features justified by real demand.
