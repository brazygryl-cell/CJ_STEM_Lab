Planned Pilot • New Jersey

One polished unit. A small teacher cohort. Evidence before expansion.

The first CJ STEM Lab pilot is being planned as a focused classroom test of one complete Grades 6–8 unit. The goal is to learn whether the materials reduce preparation burden, fit real schedules, and help students produce the intended evidence of learning.

See the Pilot DesignPilot Interest

Founding Educator Pilot

Designed to answer practical classroom questions first.

Rather than testing the entire long-term platform at once, the first pilot will focus on one complete unit with the teacher guide, slides, student resources, assessment, rubric, and standards map ready to use.

5–10 teachersacross two or more NJ school contexts
2–4 instructional weeksshort enough to implement and study well
1 complete unitGrade 6, 7, or 8

What participating educators would receive

A complete instructional package and a direct support loop.

01Complete unit

Teacher guide, daily plans, instructional slides, student activities, assessment, rubric, and standards map.

02Orientation

A concise walkthrough of the unit structure, materials, pacing, evidence expectations, and support process.

03Midpoint support

A scheduled check-in and rapid issue channel to catch confusing directions, pacing problems, or missing supports while the unit is still being taught.

04Closing feedback

Structured survey/interview questions about preparation time, usability, student response, assessment quality, and recommended revisions.

05Recognition

Free pilot access, early-adopter recognition, and professional-learning documentation where a participating school permits it.

06Early voice

Founding educators help identify what should be revised before more software features or broader sales are added.

What we would study

Implementation evidence—not vanity metrics.

The pilot is intended to document whether teachers can use the unit successfully and whether student work demonstrates the intended learning.

Preparation timeLesson completionPacing changesStudent workRubric evidenceTeacher claritySupport requests

Low-risk technology

Keep the first pilot simple.

The initial design favors teacher access and low-risk student links or codes where needed. Unnecessary student accounts and personally identifiable information are intentionally avoided until privacy, security, accessibility, consent, and deletion processes are ready.

Who the pilot is intended for

New Jersey middle-school educators working where the implementation gap is real.

Strong future pilot partners include Grades 6–8 STEM, technology, computer literacy, or computer science teachers; schools with one technology teacher serving multiple grades; districts revising curriculum; charter schools developing STEM pathways; and classrooms relying on existing Chromebooks and practical material budgets.

Grades 6–8

Middle-school implementation is the first product focus.

New Jersey first

Local standards, school structures, and educator relationships anchor the first validation cycle.

Real classroom schedules

Short periods, shortened days, rotations, and mixed readiness are useful evidence—not inconveniences to hide.

Constructive feedback

The best early partner is willing to document what worked, what did not, and what needs to change.
