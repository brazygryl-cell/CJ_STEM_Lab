For school & district leaders

A middle-school pathway that can fit your schedule without becoming a script.

CJ STEM Lab is being designed to help schools create consistent Grades 6–8 STEM, computer literacy, and computer science experiences while giving teachers the practical materials and flexibility needed for local implementation.

Flexible implementation

One curriculum core. Multiple ways to schedule it.

The platform is intended to support different staffing and timetable realities rather than requiring one specific course structure.

Dedicated course

Full-year or semester implementation with complete scope, sequence, projects, and assessments.

Quarter / rotation

Priority units and modular experiences for specials or exploratory schedules.

Enrichment

Selected units and challenges for clubs, intervention, enrichment, or after-school programs.

Interdisciplinary

Targeted units that connect computer science, design thinking, science, literacy, and career-ready skills.

Visible rigor

Standards, learning targets, and student evidence stay connected.

The first New Jersey pathway is being mapped to applicable NJSLS Computer Science and Design Thinking, Career Readiness, Life Literacies and Key Skills, and selected Science connections. Units are designed to show not only what standard appears, but what student work demonstrates learning.

Access, privacy & procurement posture

District readiness starts before student accounts exist.

This visual-build phase is a static public website and does not collect student data. Student accounts, rostering, analytics, and district integrations are future capabilities that will only be introduced after the required privacy, security, accessibility, deletion, and governance processes are ready.

Accessibility

WCAG 2.1 AA is treated as a product design requirement, including keyboard use, readable structure, contrast, captions/transcripts, and accessible documents.

Data minimization

Future product phases should collect only information required for instruction, support, security, billing, or documented improvement.

Role boundaries

Teacher, student, school, district, support, and developer permissions will be separated before account-based features launch.

No ad model

Behavioral advertising and the sale of student data are not part of the proposed business model.

Future roadmap

Build only what classroom evidence and customer need justify.

The roadmap deliberately starts with curriculum. Interactive student and district-level software comes later, after the instructional system is stable.

Current direction

Teacher curriculum hub

Structured courses, unit pages, standards views, lesson resources, downloads, search, filters, support, and eventually teacher subscriptions.

Future

Interactive student learning

Class access, assignments, selected interactive activities, coding practice, submissions, feedback, reflection, and portfolios after privacy and accessibility readiness.

Future

District system

Rostering priorities, administrator roles, reporting, retention controls, local curriculum mapping, and integrations only when customer demand and maintenance capacity justify them.

Start small, learn well

The planned first proof point is one complete unit—not a districtwide software rollout.

A small New Jersey educator cohort can provide better implementation evidence than a large number of unstructured free accounts. The planned pilot will focus on usability, pacing, preparation time, student work, and what needs revision before broader school conversations.
