The implementation gap

Standards tell teachers what students should know. They do not plan Tuesday’s lesson.

Middle-school STEM and technology teachers are still expected to turn broad outcomes into daily slides, examples, activities, assessments, differentiation, pacing changes, and project support—often across several courses at once.

Planning burden

Complete lessons, visuals, activities, and assessments live in one coherent sequence.

Different schedules

Materials are designed to adapt to 45-minute periods, shortened days, rotations, semesters, and full-year courses.

Varied teacher backgrounds

Teacher notes, worked examples, anticipated misconceptions, and facilitation guidance support content confidence.

A complete teacher system

More than activities. More than a slide deck.

Each course is designed as an instructional system so teachers can see what to teach, how to teach it, what students should produce, and how learning will be assessed.

01Daily lesson plans

Objectives, standards, vocabulary, materials, preparation, bell ringers, instruction, practice, closure, and extension.

02Instructional slides

Teacher-ready visual sequences with explicit teaching, examples, guided practice, and classroom discussion.

03Student materials

Notes, investigations, coding tasks, design briefs, reflection prompts, and low-tech alternatives.

04Assessments

Checks for understanding, rubrics, performance tasks, answer materials, exemplars, and revision opportunities.

05Differentiation

Scaffolds, multilingual supports, accessibility considerations, reteaching guidance, and enrichment options.

06Flexible pacing

Support for full-year, semester, rotation, club, early-dismissal, and shortened-period implementation.

Built in the classroom

Teacher-built by design

Curriculum shaped by real middle-school constraints.

CJ STEM Lab grows from direct experience teaching middle-school STEM, computer literacy, computer science, and advanced programming. The product is built around short periods, changing calendars, Chromebooks, mixed readiness levels, hands-on projects, and the practical question teachers ask every day: “Can I actually teach this tomorrow?”
